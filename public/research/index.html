Independent Research in Cycling Science

HamsterSpin conducts independent scientific research focused on the quantitative analysis of cycling movement, pedaling strategies, and human performance.

Our research combines real-world cycling measurements, statistical analysis, mathematical modeling, and computational methods to better understand how cyclists adapt their movements to changing workloads.

01 — Researcher

Researcher

Masahiro Fukuda, PhD (Medicine)

Principal Researcher, HamsterSpin
PhD in Medicine, Kansai Medical University, Japan (2026)

Masahiro Fukuda conducts independent research on cycling pedaling techniques, pedal force measurement, and quantitative evaluation of human movement.

His doctoral research developed a principal component analysis (PCA) framework for characterizing individual pedaling strategies using pedal force vector data collected during outdoor cycling.

02 — Research Areas

Research Areas

Pedaling Strategy Analysis

Quantifying how cyclists adapt force application throughout the crank cycle in response to changing workloads.

Pedal Force Measurement

Analyzing tangential and radial pedal forces to understand individual movement characteristics and coordination.

Statistical and Computational Modeling

Developing reproducible analytical methods using principal component analysis, regression techniques, and computational models.

Measurement Systems

Investigating sensor-based methods for accurately measuring pedal motion and cycling-related movement.

03 — Research Collaboration

Research Collaboration

Masahiro Fukuda continues to collaborate with Tomoki Kitawaki of Kansai Medical University, a co-author of multiple scientific publications on cycling pedaling analysis.

This ongoing collaboration builds upon their previous research and explores further opportunities to advance quantitative methods for evaluating cycling movement.

HamsterSpin operates independently, and this research collaboration does not represent a formal institutional affiliation with Kansai Medical University.

05 — Current Research & Future Directions

Current Research & Future Directions

Our ongoing research seeks to improve the reproducibility, interpretation, and applicability of quantitative pedaling strategy indicators.

Potential next steps include evaluating the stability of PCA-based characteristics across different riding conditions, comparing measurement approaches, and investigating computational methods for analyzing newly collected cycling data.

We aim to continue developing scientifically reproducible analytical workflows and pursue additional research publications.
